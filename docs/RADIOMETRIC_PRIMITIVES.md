# Raw radiometric primitives

This internal primitive slice implements scalar accumulation, checkpoint serialization, and raw scalar EXR output. It does not connect a rasterizer, certify any source/material/scene units, change wavelength sampling, or establish calibrated UV/IR rendering. The dedicated renderer adapter and calibration gates in [HyLIoS P6b](HYLIOS_WORKPLAN.md#82-raw-bands-and-derived-display-p6b) remain required.

## Quantity and identity

Every committed trial is a nonnegative, fully path-compensated estimate of the outgoing spectral radiance density at one fixed wavelength, in `W m^-2 sr^-1 nm^-1`. There is no wavelength draw, CIE factor, Y normalization, bin width, alpha, exposure, display transform or OIDN operation. Zero trials enter the same denominator as nonzero trials. These primitives cannot infer whether a caller's number has these units.

`RadiometricIdentity` carries width/height, pixel aspect ratio, wavelength in nm, fixed target count, exact uint64 seed, a caller-defined indexed-trial seed algorithm/revision, and an opaque canonical CBOR map of provenance. Its canonical encoding fixes observable/units/measure/schema and the `compensated_welford_scaled_delta_square_v3` accumulator revision. Identity, checkpoint and raw-plane record tags are explicitly v3; earlier staged v1/v2 checkpoints are rejected and have no compatibility migration. All fields, including exact provenance bytes, must match on resume. Pixel aspect ratio is stored exactly as float64 in identity/EXR custom metadata; OpenEXR's standard pixelAspectRatio is float32. The caller must retain resolved camera/region/configuration, source calibration and geometry scale, source data/build identity, sampler revision and exclusions in provenance; an empty or synthetic map is not a calibration certificate.

Sample generation belongs to the later adapter. It must be replayable from `(identity.seed, plane, pixel, nextSampleIndex)` using its declared algorithm; no worker-local RNG continuation is saved here. A committed index advances exactly once. A canceled trial that has not reached Commit contributes nothing and keeps its index for replay. Increasing target spp changes identity and is deliberately refused as resume compatibility in v3.

## Accumulator and failure behavior

`RadiometricFilm::Create` allocates a bounded grid. Commit, Cancel, Resume and Snapshot serialize under a mutex; snapshots are internally consistent, and one atomic commit updates count/index/mean/M2/meanCorrection/m2Correction. This is a correctness-first primitive; later batching may remove per-trial lock overhead under the same contract. Callers serialize the larger renderer/publication lifecycle themselves.

A failed numeric commit invalidates the entire film, preserves the first reason and leaves that pixel unchanged. No sample is skipped/renormalized. Wrong index/budget/bounds are also fatal while running. Lifecycle refusals after cancellation/completion preserve that result. Explicit Invalidate is available for upstream transport failures. Invalid films cannot resume or serialize as valid checkpoints/results.

Accumulator state is normalized compensated binary64 pairs: `mean + meanCorrection` and `M2 + m2Correction`. Counts are uint64 restricted to 2^53 for exact conversion. Each update recenters the mean pair using error-free TwoSum nodes and FMA-corrected division; there is no permanent first-sample anchor that could swallow a later representable decrement. The sample delta is computed against both mean components, preserving adjacent-value fluctuations and the residual needed by later samples. Every primitive add/subtract/multiply/divide node is materialized through volatile binary64 values so production reassociation cannot erase the residual; FMA captures multiplication/division residuals. The transforms assume the standard IEEE round-to-nearest-even environment and do not rely on wider long double on Apple arm64. Nearest-even is a caller precondition; these primitives do not inspect or change the floating-point environment.

M2 increments by `delta^2*(oldCount/newCount)`. frexp/scalbn keep the two-component squared delta in exponent form while bounded normal mantissas are multiplied by the compensated count ratio. The increment is then combined with the prior M2 at a common exponent before restoring physical scale. This avoids rounded-new-mean cancellation, premature square range loss and false invalidation when only a tiny auxiliary increment is unrepresentable beside an existing normal M2. The required total M2 and derived variances decide range validity; a zero-prior positive total that underflows still invalidates. A tiny term lost relative to the common scale is finite auxiliary rounding, and its trial still updates mean/count/index. M2 addition is compensated to retain small updates within the residual precision at the common scale; it does not claim to retain arbitrarily small auxiliary terms. Validation requires both pairs to be finite, normalized and nonnegative, with zero residuals on first-sample state where appropriate.

A nonfinite, out-of-range or positive-to-zero required mean/M2/variance invalidates the measurement without advancing the failed sample. Compensation is still finite precision: auxiliary residual terms can round below binary64 range, and the stored pairs are not exact-real histories or an error certificate. The mean is the high binary64 component of its normalized compensated pair. Each variance is resolved independently from the exact dyadic value represented by M2+itsCorrection and its exact count denominator. A private bounded base-2^32 integer resolver extracts the final binary64 quotient and remainder and applies nearest-even rounding once. It has no external multiprecision dependency or reliance on long-double width. In particular, mean variance is never computed from an already rounded sample variance: at n=3, M2=9*TRUE_MIN produces sample variance 4*TRUE_MIN and mean variance 2*TRUE_MIN; M2=3*TRUE_MIN has a half-minimum mean variance that rounds to even zero and is refused.

The commit/snapshot path validates required variance availability with a conservative exponent proof in ordinary ranges and an exact comparison against half-TRUE_MIN near underflow, rather than resolving both full quotients on each trial. The public statistics accessor performs the full exact rounding. When a final physical M2 lies at subnormal spacing, its combined high/low dyadic value is also rounded once; scaling the two components separately would erase a low component deciding a halfway case. Residuals below the minimum binary64 quantum remain finite-precision auxiliary loss. None of these operations reconstructs information already lost by earlier moment accumulation. Sample variance is M2/(n-1), estimated variance of the mean M2/[n(n-1)] for n>=2; n=0/1 report unavailable. For two adjacent samples separated by 2^-52, M2 is 2^-105 and estimated mean variance is 2^-106. Population two-point variance divided by two would be 2^-107, a different observable. Stored variance is an MC estimate, not a claim about independent sampling, Gaussian confidence, source calibration error or quadrature error.

Either sign of exact zero mean requires exact zero M2: nonnegative trials can reach a zero mean only through all-zero trials when positive-to-zero mean loss is refused. This invariant is checked by bit-safe zero predicates on decode, Restore and live updates. Validation is structural, not a certificate that every positive-mean tuple is a realizable history. In exact arithmetic nonnegative histories obey M2 <= n(n-1)*mean^2, from sum(x_i^2) <= sum(x_i)^2. Applying that bound directly to rounded Welford mean/M2 could reject valid states; a safe floating certificate needs error/interval information that v3 does not retain, and generic n*u forward-error bounds become vacuous near the supported 2^53 count limit. No empirical slack or complete positive-mean history-realizability claim is made.

## Real checkpoint format

`RadiometricCheckpoint` serializes Running, Cancelled or Complete snapshots. It refuses Invalid/failure state. The file contains:

* 16 bytes `RISE-RAD-CKPT3\r\n`, followed by little-endian uint64 header length.
* At most 65536 bytes of canonical RISE-CBOR64 header, with exact closed keys: record_kind, schema_version, identity (canonical byte string), identity_sha256, pixel_count, pixel_encoding and status.
* Exactly width*height records of 48 bytes: little-endian uint64 count, uint64 nextSampleIndex, then IEEE float64 mean, M2, meanCorrection and m2Correction bits. The encoding is `le_u64_u64_f64_f64_f64_f64_v3`; the header record is `radiometric_checkpoint_v3`, schema_version 3.
* 64 ASCII lowercase hex bytes for SHA256 of every preceding byte.

The checksum detects accidental corruption; it is not authentication against an attacker able to rewrite the digest. Schema/identity/status/length/state validation still runs after hash validation. Expected identity is supplied externally, not trusted from the file. Unknown or extra schema keys, schema/type changes, legacy v1/v2 framing, mismatched seed/config/build/provenance, false completion, count/index inconsistency, invalid numeric state and trailing/truncated payloads are refused before replacing an output snapshot.

Default pixel-state allocation cap is 256 MiB, checkpoint file cap 512 MiB, provenance cap 16 KiB. All size products are checked before pixel allocation. The small generic CBOR header decoder is capped separately to limit attacker-controlled tree expansion. These are per-container/file limits, not a claim that total peak resident memory is below either cap: snapshots, codec buffers, canonical CBOR trees and hashing/atomic helper resources coexist. Callers can lower budgets. Encode/load return errors on allocation/IO failures handled by their codec boundaries.

`SaveRadiometricCheckpoint` uses existing `DiskFileWriteBuffer::WriteAtomically` with Required staging. Publication uses a complete sibling then rename; it never falls back to direct truncation. Existing trusted-parent/permission policy applies. The caller must serialize writes to the same destination/aliases. If encoding completed but rename/publication subsequently fails, the shared helper deliberately retains the complete `.rise-write-*/output` sibling for recovery and leaves the prior destination unchanged under Required rename publication. Both checkpoint and EXR wrappers include the helper's absolute recovery path in the returned diagnostic. Callers may inspect/recover the complete bytes and explicitly remove that retained artifact when it is no longer needed; it is not a successful publication. Earlier failures and successful publications normally clean their staging paths. Atomic rename is not a claim of power-loss durability/fsync. Reads use one opened stream, exact bounded length, integrity and externally expected identity. Resume restores exact stored double bits and next index; it does not reconstruct RNG state or skip samples.

## Raw EXR

`WriteRadiometricEXR` publishes COMPLETE snapshots only. It uses `Imf::OutputFile`, a single FLOAT channel `L_lambda`, ZIP lossless compression, row streaming, and the existing OStreamWrapper for IWriteBuffer IO. There is no RGB/alpha channel, premultiplication, color conversion, chromaticities or white-luminance claim. Metadata includes the v3 plane/accumulator tags, wavelength, units, fixed-ordinate measure, identity/provenance hashes, exact seed/count strings and seed algorithm. It stores absolute positive values above HALF range and FLOAT subnormals. A finite ordinate above FLOAT_MAX or a positive ordinate rounding to zero is explicitly rejected before publication; no clipping/saturation. Float32 quantization resolves the full compensated mean: when its high component lies exactly on a float32 midpoint, the low component selects the correct neighboring float, avoiding a double-rounding error. This includes a value just above half the smallest FLOAT subnormal. Range refusal likewise considers a positive residual at exact FLOAT_MAX. The remaining cases use the normal cast; no magnitude clamp is introduced. NO_EXR_SUPPORT returns an explicit refusal.

Writer/codec functions take immutable snapshots and return a failure plus diagnostic; they cannot mutate the original film. A renderer adapter must treat numeric/output representability refusal as measurement failure (call Invalidate on its live film/run), and IO failure as failed publication; neither is a successful or complete output artifact. There is no automatic JSON sweep manifest or cross-file transaction in this primitive slice. The later adapter must publish plane digests and sweep status atomically and retain provenance records, since hashes alone do not recover those records.

## Minimal subsequent adapter seam

1. Build/validate a certified immutable NM render pass, units/coverage/config/build identity and indexed sampler. Create one film per fixed wavelength.
2. Generate each complete scalar trial through `PathTracingIntegrator::IntegrateRayNM`, then Commit at the current per-pixel index. Do not use the beauty film or any color conversion. Route upstream errors through Invalidate.
3. On cancellation, stop/finish outstanding trials and join workers, then Cancel and SaveRadiometricCheckpoint. Snapshot already serializes with commits, but joining establishes the renderer's whole-pass state.
4. Load with the independently reconstructed expected identity; Restore, then Resume after revalidating the scene/sources. A numeric-invalid run never resumes.
5. When Complete, WriteRadiometricEXR; only a successful publication can be included in the complete sweep manifest. Mark numeric export refusal invalid. Generate any preview separately.

The three implementation files and five headers (including private `RadiometricPublication.h` and `RadiometricExactResolve.h`) are registered in all five authoritative Library build projects with their normal production flags. The standalone `RadiometricPrimitivesTest` is discovered by the make and Windows CMake test rules; its schedule entry uses the ordinary pool with a 10-second ordering estimate, not a measured resource profile. No public API, GUI control, parser or renderer adapter is provided by these primitives.

## Focused gate

The private primitive gate passed 807 checks in strict (`-O2 -Wall -pedantic`), production (`-O3 -ffast-math -fno-finite-math-only`) and ASan/UBSan modes, and 763 checks with `NO_EXR_SUPPORT`; no compiler warnings or sanitizer diagnostics were reported in those focused gates. The gate compiled the primitive sources/tests plus existing `RISECBOR64.cpp` and `DiskFileWriteBuffer.cpp` helpers, linking the remainder from a copied stable test archive. Sanitizer coverage included those sources and helpers, while the remaining archive and system OpenEXR were uninstrumented. This is focused primitive evidence, not a clean cross-platform build, renderer validation or calibration certificate.

After normal integration, build the standalone test with `make -C build/make/rise build-test/RadiometricPrimitivesTest` and run `./bin/tests/RadiometricPrimitivesTest`. Windows CMake discovers the same test automatically; use the repository's canonical test configuration/build commands.

Independently generated exact one-high/rest-zero histories additionally check mean, M2 and estimated variance at large counts, with both residuals preserved across resume. `tests/radiometric_moment_oracles.py` derives those references from exact sums and squared sums rather than the implementation recurrence.

The public resolver's result is correct rounding of the stored moment representation, not a claim of exact original sample-history moments.
