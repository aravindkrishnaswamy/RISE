## 1. Complexes: spatial traversal, occupancy and interaction count

**What the sources specify.** Complexes use spherical envelopes and the outer geometric coefficient `(S/V)*v/4`, with `S/V=3/r_s`. Light/moderate specimens use envelope diameters respectively twice/equal to the melanosome major axis. A complex encounter has at most `n_m` internal melanosome interactions, with `n_m` given by envelope diameter divided by melanosome minor axis. Each interaction includes orientation selection and the previously described absorption test. Algorithm 1 is applied when the complex survives. Paper §4.2.1, pp. 31:6–31:7; thesis §4.2.1, pp. 27–29. The nearby spheroid formula explicitly uses **semi-axes**; the count prose does not supply an integer conversion rule.

**Author questions.** Do major/minor axes in the diameter/count prose mean full axes or semi-axes, and how is the noninteger ratio converted to `n_m`? Please give the integer for the appendix's `0.41 × 0.17 µm` light dimensions. Are children a spatial packing, sequential spatial proposals, or independent nonspatial trials? Specify child centres, filling fraction, gap lengths, containment/overlap rules, and whether predecessor exclusion also applies inside complexes. Does actual envelope exit stop interactions before the count; what happens at the count while still inside; and does a reflection consume that count? What absorbs between children, is there an envelope optical interface, and is Algorithm 1 applied once per complex or per child? A single-complex pseudocode example would resolve these jointly.

## 2. The final forward perturbation can point into the previous particle

**What the sources specify.** Paper §4.2.1 and thesis pp. 27–28 explicitly condition the **internally reflected cosine perturbation** to remain inside the spheroid. Their later Algorithm 1 draws the final forward angle over `[0,π]`; the inspected passage supplies no corresponding outward-normal rejection. Crucially, Fig. 7/Fig. 4.4 also describes discarding a surviving scattered particle before generating the next one. This supports asking whether the model intentionally treats particles as temporary virtual objects; it does not establish how predecessor exclusion and the discarded object's former interior interact.

**Questions.** At what position and relative to which direction is Algorithm 1 applied: before exit Fresnel, after refracted exit, or at a virtual event whose geometry has already been discarded? If the result points into the just-left spheroid, is it accepted as host travel after deletion, resampled outward, or physically tracked back inside? If tracked, is a new host-to-particle Fresnel test performed, and can Algorithm 1 be applied again at a later exit? What predecessor state survives deletion?

|---|---|---|
| `hylios_discard_after_scatter_v1` | Keep the full Algorithm 1 angular law; after the angular event, retain the old shape only for predecessor exclusion and continue through host even if the direction points into its former volume. This makes the virtual-particle interpretation explicit, but is not a persistent spatial particle medium. | First-sphere reentry frequency must match the unconditioned angular integral; no extra particle absorption in the discarded volume; exact host-path accounting and predecessor-retention fixtures. |

The current diagnostic preserves live weight. Our independent matched-sphere control gives `P(inward | theta)=(1-cos(theta))/2` and, for angular scale `t`, `P(inward)=[1-(1+t²)/(1+4t²)*tanh(π/(2t))]/2`. At 5°, this is about **1.10855% per first sphere encounter**. This is a derived control, not a publication result or specimen-level probability.

## 3. Layer-wall rejection and the meaning of the sampled distance

**What the sources specify.** Paper Eq. (1)/thesis Eq. (4.1) draws competing free paths and layer crossings take priority. Paper §4.2.1/thesis p. 27 then rejects spheroids that overlap their predecessor or cross a layer boundary. The inspected text does not say which parts of the event proposal are regenerated, or define a rejection-conditioned replacement-distance law.

**Questions.** Does the sampled distance end at particle entry, centre, projected plane, or another reference? After rejecting geometry, is distance held fixed, fully redrawn from the old ray origin, or consumed as a null event followed by a new flight from the candidate position? Are other absorber clocks/host absorption retained or restarted? How does the implementation finish when every geometry at the sampled event is inadmissible, and how is the stated volume fraction related to the resulting accepted encounter rate near walls?

Under the current **entry-distance convention**, a sphere of radius `r` entered by a downward vertical ray at depth `z>H-r` cannot fit in a layer ending at `H`, because its centre is at or below `z`. This counterexample does not establish the authors' convention.

- `FixedEventDiagnosticV1` already names frozen-event resampling, with definite impossibility or caller-budget exhaustion left unresolved. It is useful as a comparison control; indefinite retry must not be turned into physical absorption.
- `NullCollisionPlacementV1` already names consuming one invalid geometry proposal at its event position and continuing host clocks from there. It terminates near-wall rejection loops and preserves explicit accounting, while changing the accepted process; authored fraction is nominal, not verified occupied fraction.
- `hylios_stationary_layer_pack_v1` would instead supply a ray-independent particle layout clipped by whole-particle containment, with actual gaps/intersections replacing Eq. (3) as the encounter generator. This has a clearer spatial medium and direct occupied-volume diagnostics, at higher storage/cost and with an explicit departure from the paper's on-the-fly rate construction. Validate against brute-force geometry, analytic dilute-sphere chords, volume fractions, and source/detector swaps on the same layout.

Simply redrawing complete exponential proposals until one fits is not a finished fourth rule: the event/no-event/interface mass and competing absorption law must first be derived for that conditioning. Likewise, no generic `1/(1-v)` rate correction follows from the cited equations.

## Reciprocity and acceptance boundary

Each choice defines a different forward kernel. Predecessor memory, placement conditioning, child caps, fiber-pass state and angular perturbation are not made reciprocal by individual Fresnel tests. Stationary geometry helps define reversal but does not prove the internal scattering law reciprocal.

A future selected version needs explicit augmented state, direction/area/solid-angle measures, branch/rejection normalizers, index factors and reversed transitions. Validate paired source/detector experiments on the same layout or a precisely defined ensemble, with uncertainty. If reversal differs, derive the adjoint or retain forward measurement/light transport; do not reuse the forward kernel as camera PT or silently symmetrize it. Implementation, author reproduction and empirical fidelity remain separate gates.
